Sign language recognition (SLR) translates visual gestures into text or speech and can narrow the communication gap between signers and non-signers. In India, about 18 million deaf people depend on Indian Sign Language (ISL), yet government, healthcare and digital services rarely support it. Recognising a whole sentence of signs (continuous SLR, or CSLR) is much harder than recognising single signs, and doing so in real time adds constraints of latency, device variation and signer variation. This paper reviews AI-based approaches to continuous ISL recognition and gives a detailed, critical study of SignFlow, a real-time CSLR network proposed by Geetha et al. (IEEE Access, 2025). SignFlow combines a mixed-convolution 3D ResNet (MC3_18) for spatial features with an 8-layer Transformer encoder for temporal modelling, trained end to end with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC). Both components are pre-trained on ISL data, the CNN on isolated word videos and the Transformer on MediaPipe pose and hand features, and the input pipeline uses alignment correction and similarity-based down-sampling to 6 frames per second. We describe the datasets, architecture, training strategy, metrics (Word Error Rate and the Detection Rate), ablations and deployment results reported for SignFlow, including a WER of 19 on the Continuous ISL corpus and a load test of 74 concurrent users on one GPU. We then discuss strengths, weaknesses in the evidence, applications and future research directions. This is a review: all SignFlow results are those reported by its original authors and no new experiments are claimed.
